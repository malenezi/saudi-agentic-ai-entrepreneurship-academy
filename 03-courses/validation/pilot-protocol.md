# بروتوكول التجربة الأولى · Protocol for a first pilot

> **Before a cohort, one store:** the [pilot zero protocol](pilot-zero.md) tests the agent and the integration in a single real online store in shadow mode. This file tests the teaching.

> **No pilot has been run.** This file is the plan for one. It says what a delivering organisation should collect so that a first cohort produces evidence the Academy can act on, and how to send it back. The [desk walkthrough](desk-walkthrough.md) is not a substitute.

## 1. What a pilot must answer

| Question | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Do the hours hold? | Actual minutes per module against the instructor guide, logged by the trainer each session |
| Can learners do the activities from the materials alone? | Observation form: where the trainer had to explain what the lesson did not |
| Is the completion standard fair and usable? | Levels and decisions for every deliverable; a second assessor on one in five and on borderline cases |
| Is the scarce resource available? | How many learners secured a host business, five interviewees, a two-week deployment and graduation evidence, and how |
| Do the quizzes teach? | Scores per question; the question most learners missed in each module |
| Where is the content wrong on the day? | Every official page that differed from a lesson, with the date |
| Do learners leave with something real? | Deliverables completed; LOIs, paying customers or sustained usage, each named for what it is |

## 2. Shape of the pilot

- **One course first.** Course 1 is the most complete and needs no prior work. A full-programme pilot follows the [fifteen-week plan](../../01-academy/instructor-kit/README.md).
- **Eight to twenty learners**, in teams of two to four for cohorts, with both tracks represented if possible.
- **One trainer who has worked every activity once**, and one observer for at least half the sessions.
- **Specialists on call** for Courses 3 and 4: a lawyer, an accountant, a security practitioner. Record what they corrected; their corrections go into the [review packets](review-packets/).
- **Real businesses arranged before the first session.** A pilot that replaces them with invented ones tests nothing the desk review did not.

## 3. Consent and data

- Learners are told the cohort is a pilot and that anonymised results will be shared publicly. Participation in the survey is optional and does not affect completion.
- Nothing that identifies a learner, a host business or a customer is sent to the Academy. Send counts, minutes, levels and quotes with names removed.
- Host businesses sign the one-page agreement the courses already require before any real data is processed.
- Follow your own institution's rules for research with people where they apply.

## 4. Instruments

**Session log (trainer, every session).**

| Session | Module | Planned minutes: concepts / activity / review | Actual | Where learners got stuck | What you explained that the lesson did not | Materials that failed (link, command, slide) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | | |

**Module quiz record.** Number of learners, mean score, and the question number most learners missed.

**Learner survey (after each course, anonymous, five-point scale plus two open questions).**

1. I could do the activities with the materials provided.
2. The time given for the activities was enough.
3. The examples matched businesses I recognise.
4. I knew what a good deliverable looked like before I submitted.
5. The feedback told me exactly what to change.
6. What was the most useful thing in this course? What would you remove?

**Deliverable record.** The [completion record](../../01-academy/instructor-kit/assessment-forms.md) for each deliverable, with the second assessor's levels where there was one.

**Field access record.** For each learner or team: host business found (yes or no, and through whom), interviews completed, deployment days, graduation evidence and its kind.

## 5. Reading the results

There are no pass marks for a pilot before one has been run. Report the numbers and let them be argued over. As a starting point for discussion, look closely at any module where actual time exceeds planned time by a quarter or more, any activity most learners could not start without help, any criterion where two assessors differ by two levels, and any course where most learners are held for want of a business.

## 6. Reporting back

Open an issue titled "Pilot report: Course N, month and year" or a pull request adding `validation/pilots/<year>-<month>-<organisation>.md` with: who delivered and to whom (numbers and backgrounds, no names), the format and dates, the session log totals, the quiz record, the survey means, the deliverable decisions, the field access record, the three changes you most want, and anything in the materials that was wrong. A pilot report is published as written; the Academy does not edit findings.
